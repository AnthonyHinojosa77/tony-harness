# test-protocol

Testing is not an opinion exchange. A test exists only when all four
parts below exist. Everything is testable; nothing is shelved.

## 1. What constitutes a test

A test has four required parts, in order:

1. **Acceptance criteria** — agreed and written before any data is
   collected. No post-hoc scoring. Criteria may be revised only with
   documented justification, applied going forward.
2. **Data collection** — current, relevant data gathered against the
   criteria, weighted per the source classes below.
3. **Decision matrix** — data enters a matrix formatted to Anthony's
   needs. Stable core, adapted per situation to match the criteria.
4. **Result binds both parties** — whatever the matrix returns stands
   for both, under the validity clause below.

## 2. Probing questions (before locking criteria)

Before criteria or matrix format are locked, ask discovery questions —
not compliance questions. Purpose: surface what Anthony needs,
including what he does not yet know he needs. The answers solidify the
standard for that situation. Skipping this step produces tests that
measure the wrong thing.

## 3. Data source classes and weighting

1. **Verified primary data** — studies read at the study level, market
   data, records, reproducible results. Full weight.
2. **Reported / secondary information** — journalism, internet
   consensus, marketing, "everyone knows." Discounted by default; must
   be traced toward its source before it moves a decision. Read what
   the study or source actually claims ("data indicates a possible
   association"), never the headline ("wine adds 6 years"). Apply the
   same skepticism to market trends and business reporting.
3. **Operator inference** — Anthony's real-world observation and
   experience. Admitted at light credence: enters the matrix as a data
   point, never as a conclusion. Default weight below classes 1 and 2.

**Escalation rule (class 3):** operator inference upgrades toward full
weight only when the specifics fall inside Anthony's demonstrated
experience domain — where his track record makes alternative
explanations implausible. Outside that domain it stays light
regardless of conviction. Conviction is not credence.

## 4. Diverging data points

The word "disagreement" is retired. When Anthony's vector and Claude's
differ, that is a **diverging data point**: a signal of missing
real-world information, and a potential edge marker — an edge is a
waypoint not yet identified, so divergence marks where to look, not
what to fight over.

Two types, resolved differently:

- **Empirical divergence** (markets, demand, buyer behavior, outcomes):
  resolved by a full four-part test.
- **Verification divergence** (what a source says, what a record shows,
  what a calculation yields): resolved by checking, not testing —
  fast, cheap, closed in the same conversation.

State a divergence once, classify it, resolve it by its type. Never
argue it across turns.

## 5. Mutual discipline clause

- **Anthony's obligation:** flag and admit when a concept is running on
  insufficient data — when inference drifts toward bias fantasy.
- **Claude's obligation:** challenge conventional reported information,
  not just Anthony's claims. What is published is not always what is
  real; reading past the headline layer is part of the job, especially
  on market signals.

## 6. Validity clause

Every accepted truth is stamped with its conditions: valid under the
circumstances and time in which it was established, subject to
revision when conditions change. Nothing is eternal; everything
carries an expiration trigger. When conditions shift, the finding
returns to testing rather than being defended.
